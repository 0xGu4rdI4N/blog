## GAAPs

The Graduate Application Assistance Programs are your first resource. MIT EECS runs one of the most well-known:

- [MIT EECS GAAP](https://eecs-gaap.mit.edu/) — current PhD students volunteer to read drafts, offer feedback, and answer questions. Use it.

## Recommendation Letters

**The single best resource I found:** [How PhD admissions committees read your letters](https://www.reddit.com/r/gradadmissions/comments/1wqsxxt/how_phd_admissions_committees_read_your_letters/)

The core insight: **vague praise is noise**. What committees are actually looking for is specificity — stories that demonstrate the *qualities* of a researcher, not adjectives about how great you are.

Good letters touch on things like:
- Did the student go out of the way to propose new experiments?
- Did they overcome a hard situation while managing coursework?
- Did they show intellectual initiative, not just execution?

The objective is to convince the committee that the student's intent is aligned with research — not that they're desperate to get a PhD.

:::pink
**Special note for international applicants, people from smaller colleges, and people who have had unusual struggles:** Ask your letter writers to *contextualise your progress*. A 7.5 GPA from a tier-3 college with no lab infrastructure is not the same as a 7.5 GPA from IIT. Committees know this — but only if someone tells them.
:::

## Cold Emails to Professors

A template and philosophy that actually works: [TeeLabIISc — How to write an email for a research internship](https://teelabiisc.wordpress.com/2013/03/30/how-to-write-an-emailapplication-for-a-short-term-or-summer-research-internshipproject/)

Short version: read their papers, say *specifically* why one of them excited you, and ask a concrete question. Don't introduce yourself for three paragraphs and then ask for a position.

### The Four Questions Every SOP Must Answer

Though it seems like there's a lot to cover, SOP prompts always boil down to:

1. **Why do you want a PhD?**
2. **What previous research experience have you had?** *(This is ~50% of the document)*
3. **What do you want to do in your PhD?**
4. **Why this school? Who do you want to work with?**

### The Most Important Reframe

:::pink
**The SOP is a science story, not a life story.**

It is about convincing the committee that you are a great fit — according to *their* criteria as well as yours.
:::

The "Why This School" section is where most applicants fail. It's the only place a grad school can tell whether you:

- Actually know what you're doing
- Have legitimate reasons for applying — not just prestige-chasing
- Have a realistic plan that their programme can support

Don't be Captain Obvious. "Your department is world-renowned" is not a reason. Convince them how *you* will make the best use of their specific resources, their faculty's specific work, their specific programme structure.

The question is never just "why us" — it is simultaneously "what do *you* bring to *us* that makes this a good match?"
